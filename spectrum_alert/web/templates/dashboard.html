Getting started

Detect, then locate, identify and review. Six steps in order; each one reports whether it is ready and what is missing if it is not. Switch to guided mode to walk the steps one at a time.

  1. 1

    Add receivers

    Every receiver reports the level it hears at a known position. Triangulation needs at least two of them.

    Checking…

  2. 2

    Monitor the spectrum

    A running scan fills the charts and is what the detector, the locator and the fingerprint all read. Nothing can be found while monitoring is stopped.

    Not started

  3. 3

    Triangulate the location

    At least two receivers hearing the same signal give a fix. Click the waterfall to pick the target, then press Triangulate in the workspace.

    Waiting for step 2

  4. 4

    Identify the device

    A fingerprint reads the raw IQ stream and compares it with saved references to name the transmitter.

    Waiting for step 2

  5. 5

    Review the results

    The workspace proves the story: the fix on the map, the device verdict and the capture that produced them.

    Waiting for step 2

  6. 6

    Review anomalies

    The detector raises alerts for signals that do not fit. Work the list: locate, acknowledge or clear each one.

    Waiting for step 2

Configuration

Detection sensitivity and scan behaviour. The Save button stores Basic in this browser; the Advanced and MQTT tabs send what you record there to the server.

0.8

Built into the detector and cannot be switched off: a detection that only just clears the threshold is dropped unless its SNR is 15 dB or better.

What do these settings do?
Detection threshold
How strong a scan must score, from 0 to 1, before it is reported as an alert. Raise it for fewer, more confident alerts; lower it to catch fainter signals.
Frequency range
Used by the advanced/data-collection paths. Live scanning is tuned by the Spectrum panel's centre frequency: the receiver listens to sample-rate / 2 either side of it, not a whole band.
Scan interval
Seconds between scans while monitoring. Shorter intervals react faster and use more of the receiver.
Which of these are applied?

None of the six fields above are read back by the detector in this build. Its equivalents already run with fixed defaults: DC and edge exclusions of 5 kHz and 10 kHz, novelty filtering always on, and continuous learning handled by the command-line trainer. They are stored with the advanced and multi-band configuration so a later build can pick them up.

Multi-band configuration

Recording stores the configuration; it does not start a capture. Press Start monitoring in the Spectrum panel when you want to record signal.

● Broker settings: Not saved

Saving stores the broker, port and topic for this session and reports them to the dashboard. This build does not open a connection to the broker or publish messages.

How do these choices affect training?
Model type
Isolation Forest is the default and the fastest to train. Autoencoder trains a small reconstruction network. One-Class SVM learns a boundary around normal behaviour.
Dataset
Which recorded scans to learn from. Training needs at least ten samples.
Epochs
Training passes for the autoencoder. Higher is slower and more prone to overfitting small datasets.
Contamination
Tells the model what fraction of the training rows it should treat as outliers. Set it close to the alert rate you expect.
